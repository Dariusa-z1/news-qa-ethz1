## A rubbish-strewn utopia

From a straightforward street variety show in 1984 to an extravagant water show ( AKUA , 2002) all the way to a fight over a chocolate factory (2011): over its 30-year history, Karl’s kühne Gassenschau has established itself as a real highlight of the Swiss cabaret scene.

## Technician, astronaut, bin man

However, the term “cabaret” is something of a misnomer; the ensemble currently includes more than 100 performers specialising in a wild, expansive mixture of theatre, music and acrobatics that relies on heavy equipment and can only be staged outdoors. The current set at an industrial plot in Winterthur covers 48,000 m 2 and is dominated by a massive, 176-tonne funnel filled with 100,000 litres of water.

The Gassenschau isn’t just about sound, smoke and slapstick – the performances are also supposed to make audiences think. The troupe always chooses to examine issues relevant to contemporary audiences. The current production is set in an artificially created natural park (Sektor1) in the year 2066, where all waste is dumped into outer space. Out of sight, out of mind – that is, until the protective shield in orbit starts failing, and these apparently wholesome types find themselves once again up to their ears in trash. The utopia, which is in fact a dictatorial regime, is flipped upside down.
